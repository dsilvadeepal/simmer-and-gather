# Five hosting draft stress tests — 29 October 2026

These are isolated simulations, not saved gatherings or calendar bookings. Seed: 20261029. Each menu has five randomly selected dishes within meal-role pools. They are stress tests, not endorsed menus.

Common test assumptions: 15 people including five vegetarians; December 22–25 prep; two hours per day, three hours on serving day; 6 pm arrival; 30-minute protected buffer; no helper; standard kitchen. Availability is an empty test fixture, **not a live calendar check**. Christmas drafts request nut avoidance; unknown ingredient flags require review. Quantities use 15 servings per selected dish for scheduler exercise only, not a recommended buffet shopping quantity.

## Result

All five drafts remain incomplete under these constraints. Do not treat the scheduled subset as the full plan. This is useful evidence that recipe checks alone are insufficient: menu balance, portion allocation, storage transitions and elapsed kitchen occupancy also need review.

## Indian supper

**Menu:** Aloo Chaat in Phyllo Shells; Instant Pot Butter Chicken; Instant Pot Palak Paneer; Vegetable Pulao Recipe | Veg Pulav; Instant Pot Carrot Halwa Recipe.

**New-host critique: Too much last-minute cooking.** The chaat shells need crisp assembly near serving, both curries compete for the pressure cooker, and pulao adds another live cooking sequence. A new host would not know which dish to sacrifice when time runs out.

**Recommended change:** Buy the starter or dessert; explicitly approve and model cooking a curry ahead, including cooling and reheating. Keep the phyllo toppings for the end.

## Indian vegetarian

## Thanksgiving

**Menu:** Thanksgiving Turkey; Garlic Mashed Potatoes Recipe; Cornbread Stuffing Recipe; Cranberry Orange Sauce; Gluten Free Apple Crisp.

**New-host critique: A side is not automatically vegetarian.** The stuffing contains sausage and chicken broth. The menu has no substantial vegetarian main for five vegetarian guests. Turkey oven time and the apple crisp compete even though hands-on minutes look modest. The crisp also contains nuts.

**Recommended change:** Label stuffing as nonvegetarian; add or substitute a substantial vegetarian main. Make or buy dessert ahead; show turkey temperature changes clearly and ask whether the turkey is thawed.

## Goan Christmas

**Menu:** Easy Tuna Croquettes; Chicken Cafreal; Goan Sannas (yeast method); Sonay Sukhe (Black Chana Sukka); The Best Doce.

**New-host critique: This menu is demanding for a first-time host.** Croquettes, cafreal and sannas all need attention near serving. Sannas require fermentation and steaming; doce needs prolonged stirring. Black chana is the main vegetarian protein, so a side-sized quantity may be too little. Nut flags are unknown for several recipes; absence of a listed nut is not a confirmed allergy review.

**Recommended change:** Buy doce or sannas, increase the chana portion deliberately, and complete the nut ingredient review before offering this as an allergy-compatible menu.

## Christmas mixed table

**Menu:** Mushroom Cutlets; Pork Bafat | Dukra Maas; Sonay Sukhe (Black Chana Sukka); Caramelized Brussels Sprouts; The Best Doce.

**New-host critique: Manageable dishes can still form a difficult menu.** Cutlets need continuous frying, sprouts lose crispness when held, and chana needs an overnight soak. Doce adds substantial hands-on stirring. Pork bafat has an explicit reheat step, but the overall menu lacks a plain starch such as rice or bread.

**Recommended change:** Add bought bread or a simple rice option; buy doce. Keep frying and roasting close enough to serving to preserve texture without consuming the protected buffer.

## What to improve next

1. Run menu-level checks for vegetarian coverage, complementary dishes, optional starters/desserts and confirmed allergy information before scheduling.
2. Treat cooked-ahead storage, cooling and reheating as explicit linked steps; do not simply enlarge a preparation window.
3. Preserve no-gap sequences such as batter-to-baking and close-to-serving crisp foods.
4. Show missing work clearly; never present a feasible-looking subset as a complete plan.
5. Separate buffet quantity reasoning from the current one-batch timing assumption.
